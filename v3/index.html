Why a specialist

A children's dentist is not just a dentist who sees children

Pediatric dentistry is its own specialty. It covers how children's teeth grow, how habits shape a jaw, how to treat a nervous four-year-old without frightening them, and how to spot the problems that only show up in a growing mouth.

My training is in exactly that: a Bachelor of Dental Surgery, a Master's degree, a pediatric dentistry certificate, American Board certification and a Canadian Fellowship from the Royal College of Dentists of Canada.

What that means for your child is simple — unhurried appointments, explanations before anything happens, and a dentist who has seen this exact worry many times before.

Both can treat a child. The difference is what the training was built for.

A particular focus

Dentistry for children with special needs

Alongside pediatric dentistry, I am a consultant in dentistry for people with special needs. Many families are told their child is "too difficult" to treat. Usually what's needed is more time, a different approach, and someone trained for it.

Time, not rushing

Appointments paced to your child rather than to a schedule — including short familiarisation visits where nothing clinical happens at all.

A predictable routine

The same room, the same order of events, explained step by step beforehand, so there are as few surprises as possible.

Parents stay involved

You know your child best. Tell me what calms them and what sets them off, and we build the visit around that.

If your child has a sensory, developmental or medical condition that has made dental visits hard before, tell me about it when you book — that conversation is genuinely useful.

What I do

Care for children, from the first tooth onward

  • First visits & check-ups

    A gentle introduction to the dentist, ideally before anything hurts.

  • Fluoride & sealants

    Simple, quick preventive treatments that stop cavities before they start.

  • Fillings & restorations

    For baby and permanent teeth, done in a way children can cope with.

  • Special-needs dental care

    Consultant-level care adapted to your child's needs and tolerances.

  • Dental anxiety & behaviour guidance

    For the child who has had a bad experience, or has never been at all.

  • Growth & habit monitoring

    Thumb-sucking, late or crowded teeth, and how a bite is developing.

  • Preventive advice for parents

    Brushing, diet and fluoride guidance that fits a real family routine.

  • Dental emergencies

    Knocked-out, broken or painful teeth — call as soon as you can.
